### Thermodynamic and Genetic Resource Distribution Analysis

#### Thermodynamic Perspective

1. **Energy Allocation and Resource Scarcity:**
   - **Initial Conditions:** Under extreme localized resource scarcity, the energy budget of a population is constrained. The primary energy allocation is towards survival and reproduction.
   - **Energy Prioritization:** Military-aged males, being at peak physical condition, are prioritized for migration due to their potential to secure resources and establish new territories.
   - **Energy Expenditure:** The energy cost of migration includes physical exertion, risk of injury or death, and the establishment of new settlements. This is a high-risk, high-reward scenario driven by the need to optimize genetic fitness over multiple generations.

2. **Macro-Demographic Shifts:**
   - **Non-Linear Dynamics:** The migration of a significant portion of the male population (exceeding 8% annually) leads to a rapid decline in the domestic state's tax base and defense budgets.
   - **Velocity Collapse:** The rate of economic contraction is non-linear, accelerating as the labor force diminishes. This creates a feedback loop where reduced economic stability further incentivizes migration.

#### Evolutionary-Biology Perspective

1. **Genetic Fitness Optimization:**
   - **Reproductive Resource Constraints:** In environments with scarce resources, the ability to reproduce and ensure the survival of offspring becomes paramount. Males migrate to areas with higher resource availability to maximize reproductive success.
   - **Multi-Generational Strategy:** The migration is not just for immediate survival but for establishing a stable environment for future generations, ensuring long-term genetic fitness.

2. **Demographic Dependency Ratios:**
   - **Initial Dependency:** High dependency ratios in the originating zones due to a lack of labor force exacerbate resource scarcity.
   - **Transition Dynamics:** As males migrate, the dependency ratio initially worsens, but the potential for improved ratios exists in the target environments if successful settlement occurs.

#### Economic and Structural Analysis

1. **Economic Stability and Infrastructure:**
   - **Contraction Timeline:** The economic stability of the originating zone contracts as the labor force diminishes. This is modeled by a decrease in GDP, tax revenues, and public services.
   - **Infrastructure Degradation:** With fewer resources and labor, infrastructure maintenance declines, leading to further economic and social instability.

2. **Resource-Extraction Curves:**
   - **Originating Zone:** Resource extraction becomes less efficient, leading to a decline in available goods and services.
   - **Target Environment:** Successful migration leads to increased resource extraction efficiency, assuming the environment can support the new population.

#### Conclusion

The migration of military-aged males under conditions of extreme resource scarcity is driven by a complex interplay of thermodynamic constraints and evolutionary imperatives. The energy allocation towards migration is a strategic investment in genetic fitness, while the economic and demographic impacts on the originating zone are severe and non-linear. The success of such migrations depends on the ability to establish sustainable settlements in resource-rich environments, ensuring long-term demographic security and genetic optimization.
