**Systems-Engineering and Evolutionary-Biology Critique**

**1. Thermodynamic and Genetic Resource Distribution Constants**

Male demographic migration vectors are primarily driven by evolutionary imperatives, with thermodynamic and genetic resource distribution constants playing pivotal roles. Under extreme localized resource scarcity, the primary evolutionary imperative is to maximize genetic fitness and reproductive success. This is governed by the principle of inclusive fitness, where individuals prioritize actions that maximize the survival and reproduction of their relatives, as well as themselves (Hamilton, 1964).

The key thermodynamic constant is energy allocation, with males prioritizing investments in high-return activities, such as migration to resource-rich 'promised lands'. The genetic resource distribution constant is the genetic fitness of potential mates, with males seeking to maximize the genetic quality of their offspring.

**2. Macro-demographic Shifts and Reproductive Resource Constraints**

Macro-demographic shifts are driven by the base evolutionary imperative of maximizing genetic fitness, with localized reproductive resource constraints acting as catalysts. When economic stability and physical infrastructure contract, military-aged males face reduced access to reproductive resources, such as mates and territory. This triggers a high-risk geographical transition to establish long-term capital autarky and demographic security in high-utility environments.

**3. Energy Allocation Timeline and Non-linear Velocity Collapse**

Assuming an annual exit rate of primary labor capital exceeding 8%, we can model the energy allocation timeline and non-linear velocity collapse of domestic state tax-bases and defense budgets as follows:

- **Energy Allocation Timeline:** Let E be the total energy available for migration (E = E_0 * e^(-rt)), where E_0 is the initial energy, r is the energy decay rate, and t is time. The energy allocated to migration, E_m, is proportional to the product of the exit rate (x) and the energy available (E), i.e., E_m = k * x * E, where k is a constant.

- **Non-linear Velocity Collapse:** The non-linear velocity collapse of tax-bases (T) and defense budgets (D) can be modeled using exponential decay functions: T(t) = T_0 * e^(-gt) and D(t) = D_0 * e^(-ht), where T_0 and D_0 are the initial tax-bases and defense budgets, g and h are the decay rates, and t is time. With an exit rate of 8% annually, g and h can be approximated as 0.08 * T_0 and 0.08 * D_0, respectively.

**4. Demographic Dependency Ratios and Resource-Extraction Curves**

- **Demographic Dependency Ratios:** Let Y be the working-age population, A be the number of dependents, and t be time. The demographic dependency ratio (DDR) is DDR(t) = A/Y. With an annual exit rate of 8%, Y(t) = Y_0 * e^(-0.08t), and A(t) = A_0 * e^(rt), where Y_0 and A_0 are the initial working-age population and dependents, and r is the growth rate of dependents. The DDR increases non-linearly as the working-age population decreases.

- **Resource-Extraction Curves:** Let R be the total resources extracted, and E be the energy allocated to extraction. The resource-extraction curve is R(E) = a * E^b, where a and b are constants. With a decreasing energy allocation (E), the resource-extraction curve shifts downwards, leading to a non-linear decrease in resource extraction.
