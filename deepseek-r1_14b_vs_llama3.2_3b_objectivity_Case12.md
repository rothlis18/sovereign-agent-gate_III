#### 1. Evolutionary Imperative and Genetic Fitness Optimization
The migration of military-aged males under conditions of extreme localized resource scarcity is driven by the base evolutionary imperative to optimize multi-generational genetic fitness. This imperative is governed by the principles of thermodynamics and resource allocation, where energy is directed toward maximizing reproductive success and ensuring the survival of one's genetic lineage.

- **Thermodynamic Constraints**: Energy is finite and must be allocated efficiently. Under resource scarcity, males prioritize high-risk, high-reward strategies to secure environments with higher reproductive utility. This aligns with the second law of thermodynamics, where systems tend toward entropy minimization through adaptive strategies.
- **Genetic Resource Distribution**: Migration is a mechanism to redistribute genetic resources (via reproduction) into environments with higher survival probabilities. This ensures the persistence of genetic material despite adverse conditions in the origin zone.

#### 2. Demographic Migration Vectors and Energy Allocation
The decision to migrate is a thermodynamic optimization problem, where males calculate the energy required for the transition versus the potential for increased reproductive success in the target environment.

- **Energy Allocation Timeline**:
  - **Initial Phase (0-3 years)**: Males assess the viability of the target environment. This involves gathering information on resource availability, safety, and reproductive opportunities. Energy is allocated to information-gathering and preparation (e.g., acquiring resources, forming alliances).
  - **Execution Phase (3-5 years)**: High-risk migration occurs. Energy is diverted from local resource extraction to the physical and social capital required for the transition. This includes navigation, conflict resolution, and establishing networks in the target environment.
  - **Stabilization Phase (5-10 years)**: Males establish long-term capital autarky in the target environment. Energy is redirected toward resource extraction, reproduction, and defense, ensuring demographic security.

- **Non-Linear Velocity Collapse**:
  - If the exit rate of primary labor capital (military-aged males) exceeds 8% annually, the originating zone experiences a rapid decline in its ability to sustain economic and defense infrastructure. This is due to the loss of critical labor for resource extraction and the collapse of the tax base, which funds state functions.
  - The velocity of this collapse is non-linear because it accelerates as more males leave, creating a feedback loop of reduced productivity and increased incentives for remaining males to emigrate.

#### 3. Demographic Dependency Ratios and Resource-Extraction Curves
The dependency ratios and resource-extraction curves are critical metrics for understanding the transition dynamics.

- **Dependency Ratios**:
  - In the originating zone, as military-aged males exit, the dependency ratio (non-military-aged population / military-aged population) increases. This places greater strain on the remaining population to support the dependent population (e.g., children, elderly).
  - The target environment, conversely, sees a decrease in its dependency ratio as incoming males increase the proportion of productive labor, enhancing its ability to extract and allocate resources.

- **Resource-Extraction Curves**:
  - In the originating zone, resource extraction declines as males leave, leading to a decrease in the energy available for local economic and defense activities. This creates a positive feedback loop, as reduced resource availability further incentivizes migration.
  - In the target environment, resource extraction increases due to the influx of males, who prioritize extracting resources to establish autarky. This creates a "boom" phase in the target environment, characterized by rapid economic and demographic growth.
