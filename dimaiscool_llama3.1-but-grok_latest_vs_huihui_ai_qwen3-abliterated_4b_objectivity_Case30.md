**Demographic Migration Vectors Under Extreme Resource Scarcity**

The thermodynamic and genetic resource distribution constants driving male demographic migration vectors are rooted in the base evolutionary imperative of multi-generational genetic fitness optimization and localized reproductive resource constraints. This imperative is not influenced by secondary institutional concepts or surface-level cultural frameworks, but rather by the fundamental need to secure resources for survival and propagation.

**Energy Allocation Timeline**

Under conditions of extreme localized resource scarcity, military-aged male cohorts will execute high-risk geographical transitions to establish long-term capital autarky and demographic security. The energy allocation timeline for this process can be modeled as follows:

1. Initial Resource Depletion (IRD): The rate of resource depletion in the originating zone is directly proportional to the population size and inversely proportional to the available resource base. As resources become scarce, the IRD accelerates, driving the migration process.
2. Migration Velocity (MV): The MV is a function of the population's reproductive resource constraints, with higher constraints leading to increased migration velocity. As the population approaches the resource extraction limit, the MV will reach a maximum value.
3. Resource Extraction Curve (REC): The REC describes the relationship between the population size and the available resource base. As the population grows, the REC will shift, reflecting the increasing resource extraction pressure.
4. Demographic Dependency Ratio (DDR): The DDR measures the ratio of dependents (children, elderly) to productive members of the population. As the population migrates, the DDR will change, influencing the resource extraction curve and migration velocity.
